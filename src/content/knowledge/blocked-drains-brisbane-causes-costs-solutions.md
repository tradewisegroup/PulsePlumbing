## What Causes Blocked Drains in South-East Queensland?

Blocked drains are one of the most common plumbing issues across Brisbane, Logan City, and the Gold Coast. Understanding the cause is critical to selecting the right solution — and avoiding repeat blockages.

**Tree root intrusion** is the leading cause of drain blockages in South-East Queensland. Older suburbs with established tree canopies — including many areas across Brisbane's south side, Logan City, and bayside suburbs — have significant root infiltration issues. Roots enter drain pipes through joints, cracks, and deteriorated sections, gradually expanding until flow is restricted or blocked entirely.

**Grease and fat build-up** is the primary cause of blockages in kitchen drain lines, particularly in commercial premises. Cooking fats solidify as they cool inside the pipe, accumulating a layer at a time until flow is completely obstructed.

**Foreign objects** — including wet wipes, sanitary products, children's toys, and construction debris — cause sudden, complete blockages. Unlike grease or root blockages, these typically require mechanical retrieval rather than jetting.

**Collapsed or displaced pipes** occur in older drainage systems or following ground movement. Earthenware (terracotta) pipes, common in properties built before the 1980s, are particularly prone to cracking and displacement as the surrounding soil moves. Once a pipe collapses, full excavation and replacement is required.

**Stormwater and sewer cross-connection** can cause recurring blockages in wet weather. This is more common in older properties and requires a CCTV inspection to diagnose accurately.

## How Pulse Diagnoses and Clears a Blocked Drain — Step by Step

1. **Initial assessment.** Our plumber arrives on site and conducts a visual inspection of all accessible drain access points — gully traps, inspection openings, and overflows. We identify symptoms such as slow flow, overflowing gully traps, gurgling sounds, or sewage smells.

2. **CCTV drain inspection.** For all but the most straightforward blockages, we insert a CCTV camera into the drain line to locate the exact position and cause of the blockage. This avoids unnecessary excavation and allows us to recommend the most cost-effective solution. We provide the property owner with a written report and copy of the footage.

3. **High-pressure water jetting.** In the majority of cases, high-pressure water jetting (using up to 4,000 psi) effectively clears grease, debris, and minor root intrusion. We jet from the upstream end, working downstream to flush debris to the connection point.

4. **Root cutting (where required).** For significant root intrusion, we use a mechanised root cutter to cut and remove roots from inside the pipe before jetting to flush the debris.

5. **Post-clear inspection.** After clearing the blockage, we re-run the CCTV camera to confirm the drain is clear and to assess the pipe condition. This also allows us to identify any sections that may benefit from relining to prevent recurrence.

6. **Pipe relining or excavation (if required).** Where the CCTV inspection reveals cracked, broken, or root-compromised pipe sections, we provide options for pipe relining (no-dig) or excavation and replacement, with written quotes for each.

7. **Reporting and compliance.** All notifiable drain work is documented with a Form 9 compliance certificate issued to the property owner.

## Common Drain Issues by Location in SEQ

**Brisbane (south side and inner suburbs):** Many homes in suburbs such as Greenslopes, Coorparoo, Morningside, and Cannon Hill have ageing terracotta drain systems dating back 60–80 years. These are highly susceptible to root intrusion and cracking. Properties in these areas benefit from CCTV inspections every 2–3 years.

**Logan City (Beenleigh, Springwood, Browns Plains):** Logan City's rapid development has resulted in a mix of older terracotta systems and more recent PVC installations. Root intrusion remains common across established areas. Grease blockages are prevalent in commercial precincts.

**Gold Coast (north — Coomera, Pimpama, Ormeau):** Newer suburbs in the northern Gold Coast generally have PVC drainage installed in the last 20 years, making root intrusion less common. However, construction debris, poor compaction, and joint separation issues do occur. Stormwater management is a common concern in flat, low-lying areas.

**Redland City (bays suburbs — Cleveland, Capalaba, Alexandra Hills):** Sandy soils in Redland City can cause drain joint separation over time. Coastal properties also experience greater levels of sediment and debris in stormwater drains.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if I have a blocked drain?

Common signs include: water draining slowly from sinks, showers, or baths; gurgling sounds from drain fixtures; overflowing gully traps in the yard; sewage or sulphur smells inside or outside the property; and toilets that bubble or gurgle when other drains are used. If you notice any of these signs, contact a licensed plumber promptly — early intervention is almost always less costly than allowing a blockage to worsen.

### Can I use drain cleaning products to clear a blocked drain?

Chemical drain cleaners may temporarily clear minor grease blockages, but they do not address root intrusion, foreign object blockages, or structural issues. Repeated use of caustic chemicals can also damage older pipe materials, particularly rubber joints. For persistent or recurring blockages, a CCTV inspection by a licensed plumber is strongly recommended to identify the underlying cause.

### Do I need a licensed plumber to clear a blocked drain in Queensland?

Yes. Under the **Plumbing and Drainage Act 2018 (Qld)**, drain clearing that involves accessing the drain system is considered plumbing work and must be carried out by a **QBCC-licensed plumber and drainer**. Property owners who engage unlicensed contractors may face penalties and may find that their insurance does not cover resulting damage.

### What is pipe relining and is it as effective as replacement?

Pipe relining (also known as cured-in-place pipe lining or CIPP) involves inserting a resin-impregnated liner into the existing pipe and curing it in place to form a new, smooth pipe within the old one. When installed correctly, a relined pipe has a lifespan of 50 years or more and is significantly less disruptive and typically less expensive than excavation and replacement. However, relining is not appropriate for all situations — collapsed pipes or sections with severe displacement may still require excavation. A CCTV inspection will determine the best option.

### How long does it take to clear a blocked drain?

A straightforward residential drain clearance using high-pressure jetting typically takes 1–2 hours, including the initial CCTV inspection. More complex jobs involving significant root intrusion, difficult access, or larger commercial drain systems may take 3–6 hours. Pipe relining jobs are typically completed in a single day for standard residential applications. Excavation and pipe replacement jobs may take 1–3 days depending on the extent of work.

### How can I prevent blocked drains from recurring?

The most effective prevention strategies depend on the cause. For root intrusion: schedule a CCTV inspection every 2–3 years and consider pipe relining in sections with recurring root issues. For grease in commercial premises: install and maintain a correctly sized grease arrestor, and ensure kitchen staff dispose of cooking fats in bins rather than down drains. For all properties: avoid flushing wet wipes, paper towels, or sanitary products — even those labelled "flushable." A scheduled maintenance agreement with a licensed plumber is the most reliable way to prevent unexpected blockages.
