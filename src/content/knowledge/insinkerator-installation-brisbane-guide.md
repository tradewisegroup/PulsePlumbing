## What Is an InSinkErator Food Waste Disposer?

An InSinkErator food waste disposer (also called a garbage disposal unit or food waste disposer) is an under-sink appliance that grinds food scraps into fine particles and flushes them into the sewerage system via the kitchen drain. InSinkErator is the leading brand in Australia — widely recognised by the brand name, though the product category includes units from multiple manufacturers.

The unit is compact, fitting entirely under the kitchen sink cabinet, and connects to the standard sink drain waste outlet. It requires both a plumbing connection to the sink waste and drain, and an electrical power source — typically a switched GPO (general purpose outlet) under the sink or a dedicated wall switch wired to a GPO.

InSinkErators are particularly popular in residential kitchens, commercial kitchens, rental properties, and aged care facilities where rapid, hygienic disposal of food scraps is important.

## Why Queensland Homeowners Are Installing InSinkErators

Food waste disposers have grown in popularity across Brisbane, Logan, and the Gold Coast for several practical reasons:

- **Eliminates food odours from bins.** Food scraps processed immediately through the disposer do not sit in the kitchen bin, eliminating the most common source of kitchen odours.
- **Reduces food waste to landfill.** Approximately 7.3 million tonnes of food waste goes to Australian landfills annually. Grinding food scraps for treatment via the sewerage system is considered a more environmentally responsible outcome than landfill disposal.
- **More hygienic kitchen management.** Particularly relevant for households with young children, commercial food preparation environments, and aged care facilities.
- **Environmental note.** Ground food waste is approximately 70% water and is effectively treated at council sewerage treatment plants. Most Queensland council sewerage authorities accept food waste via the sewerage system — confirm with your local authority if in doubt.

## Do You Need a Licensed Plumber to Install an InSinkErator in Queensland?

**Yes — absolutely and without exception.**

Under the **Plumbing and Drainage Act 2018 (Qld)** and the **Standard Plumbing and Drainage Regulation 2019**, all plumbing connection work — including connecting a food waste disposer to your sink waste and the drainage system — is classified as notifiable plumbing work. This work must be carried out by a **QBCC-licensed plumber**.

**Risks of DIY InSinkErator installation:**
- **Voided warranty.** InSinkErator's Australian warranty explicitly requires installation by a licensed plumber. DIY installation voids the manufacturer's warranty.
- **Drainage problems.** Incorrect trap and waste configuration can cause siphonage, slow drainage, or blockages.
- **Compliance issues on sale or lease.** Unlicensed plumbing work can create compliance problems when selling or leasing the property, particularly if Form 9 compliance certificates cannot be produced.

## How to Operate Your InSinkErator Correctly

1. Turn on the cold water tap with a medium flow.
2. Turn on the InSinkErator at the wall switch or air switch.
3. Gradually feed food scraps into the unit — do not overload.
4. Keep the water running for 10–15 seconds after grinding is complete to flush the drain line.
5. Turn off the InSinkErator, then turn off the tap.

### What You Can Put in an InSinkErator

Vegetable peelings, fruit peelings and pits, eggshells, coffee grounds, tea leaves, meat scraps, fish bones, prawn shells, pasta, rice, bread, and most other natural food scraps.

### What NOT to Put in an InSinkErator

**Never put the following into an InSinkErator:**
- Fats, oils and grease (FOG) — solidify in the drain and cause blockages
- Fibrous vegetables — celery, corn husks, artichokes (wrap around the grinding mechanism)
- Large bones — animal joints, pork bones
- Oyster shells, clam shells
- Glass, metal, non-food items
- Hot water — always use cold water when running the unit

## Common InSinkErator Problems and Solutions

### InSinkErator Jammed or Seized

**Cause:** A hard object — spoon, bone, fruit pit, or shell — has lodged in the grinding chamber, preventing the grinding plate from spinning.

**DIY steps:**
1. Turn off power at the wall switch.
2. Locate the self-service wrenchette (small hex key in a plastic sleeve attached to the base of the unit).
3. Insert the wrenchette into the centre hole on the bottom of the unit and work it back and forth until the grinding plate spins freely.
4. Use tongs — never your hand — to remove any object from inside the unit.
5. Press the red reset button on the base of the unit.
6. Restore power, run cold water, and test the unit.

**When to call:** If the reset fails, if the motor hums but does not spin, or if the jam recurs repeatedly, call Pulse on [0452 188 420](tel:0452188420).

### InSinkErator Leaking

InSinkErator leaks come from one of three locations:

- **Top — sink flange:** The seal between the flange and the sink has failed (putty or rubber gasket). Usually repairable by reseating the flange.
- **Side — discharge outlet:** The connection between the unit and the waste pipe is loose or the seal has worn. Usually repairable by tightening or replacing the seal.
- **Bottom of the unit:** Indicates an internal seal failure within the unit itself. This is not repairable — the unit needs replacing.

Leaks under the sink can damage your cabinetry quickly. Call Pulse on [0452 188 420](tel:0452188420) for a same-day assessment.

### InSinkErator Not Draining / Slow Drain

**Cause:** Usually a blocked S-trap under the sink or grease build-up further down the drain line — not the InSinkErator unit itself.

**DIY:** Check and clear the S-trap (the curved section of waste pipe directly connected to the disposer's discharge outlet).

**When to call:** If the S-trap is clear but the drain is still slow or backing up, the blockage is further in the drain line. Pulse can diagnose and clear this using CCTV inspection and high-pressure water jetting. [See our blocked drains service →](/services/blocked-drains)

### InSinkErator Stopped Working

**Check in sequence:**
1. Wall switch — confirm it is in the ON position.
2. Circuit breaker — check your switchboard for a tripped breaker.
3. Red reset button — locate and press the reset button on the base of the unit (it may have tripped on thermal overload).

**When to call:** If all the above checks pass and the unit still does not operate, the motor or an internal component has failed. Call Pulse for an assessment — for older units (7+ years), replacement is typically more cost-effective than repair.
